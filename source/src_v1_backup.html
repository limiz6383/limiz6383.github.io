Product work

Three product areas at Xbox Media Solutions. The problem, my call, what moved.

Agentic Creative Platform

Jun 2026 – present

~30 hours of designer work per mockup became ~10 minutes of system generation time.

That tripled how often sales could pitch an ad product worth $400K in incremental revenue, from 5 pitches a quarter to 5 a month.

The problem

Win rate on our premium ad product was sliding, and we weren't selling it as much as we wanted to.

My call

Fix how pitches get made. I dug into the sales process and tested two hypotheses: sellers had no standard way to pick the best offering for a brief, so they leaned on instinct, and limited creative bandwidth sent many pitches out without a customized mockup that showed clients the value.

How I shipped it

I prototyped both fixes myself, a strategy analyzer that picks the offering that maximizes revenue and fits the brief, and a mockup generator on a custom-trained art model, so the team could react to something real. We led with the mockup generator, timed with our new premium ad product launch, and I built an LLM-as-judge eval framework to keep every generation within the rules. Then I took both to production with engineering.

Agents · LLM-as-judge · Opportunity sizing · 0 to 1

Ad Quality

Nov 2025 – May 2026

20,000+ policy-violating creatives blocked per month, where reactive blocking caught 20–30 per day.

An ML model that blocks policy-violating ads before they reach players, across 100M+ daily impressions.

The problem

Studio partners kept raising ad quality as a threat to player retention, yet we had no baseline for how good or bad our ads were and no system to catch the policy-violating ones. Every block was reactive.

My call

Define bad before blocking it. I built the measurement framework with a data analyst: what counts as a bad ad, with technical quality separated from content quality. I specified the ad metadata we were missing so bad ads could be classified, and replaced reliance on third-party manual moderation with a system that catches them by design.

What we built

A measurement framework and dashboard that gave ad quality its first baseline. An ML model that reads ad metadata and blocks violators before players see them, duplicates included, whatever shape or form. Blocking went from 20–30 ads a day to about 20,000 a month.

Strategy, led by the data

The team's assumptions about what players cared about, and the skewed complaints we heard now and then, were pointing us the wrong way. The full complaint data showed repetitive serving as the top quality issue for 13 straight quarters, and no metric measured it. I wrote the H1 FY27 moderation strategy around that data and moved the roadmap toward technical ad quality tooling.

Trust and safety · ML flagging · Metrics design · Strategy

Trip Planner

An AI travel planner, built with Next.js, Supabase and Claude

Most planners start from a blank page. This one starts from what is already fixed in your trip and from how you actually travel, then builds a day-by-day itinerary you can reshape by talking to it.

Open the live demoRead the code

The hosted demo runs on sample data and never calls an AI API, so nobody's usage is billed. Web research and venue lookup run only in live mode with your own keys.

Screens from the product
Bookings become anchors
A flight, a concert at 8:15 pm or a dinner reservation is treated as a fixed point, and the rest of the day is built around it. Anything you lock stays put when you revise.
Local, not touristy
A dial runs from local to touristy. Community posts are ranked by how authentic the author is, and real venues come from OpenStreetMap, scored for how local they are. Star ratings are left out on purpose, because they reward whatever the most tourists already visited.
Consult the AI at every step
Talk any part of the trip through with it, and the plan only changes when you press Apply. Every apply makes a new version you can review.
Personalized from the first plan
It asks about budget, group size and travel style, and learns a style from your past trips, so the first plan already looks like yours.
Your food picks become real meals
Tick a dish in the destination guide and it shows up as a meal at a real restaurant, with several picks grouped at one venue where that makes sense.

Cycle Insights

An AI insights tool for Oura data, built in Python with Claude

Oura is not built to specialize in women's health. This tool reads your Oura data against your own baseline and your cycle stage, and turns it into a short insight with nutrition guidance, but only if it passes its own safety review.

Read the code and methodologyOpen the demo page

I built it as a personal tool for one user, and it is not a clinical device. The screens below come from the demo in the repo, which uses invented data, but the rules, sources and checks are the real ones.

Screens from the demo
Your own baseline, not an average
Most cycle apps compare you to a population norm. This reads last night's heart rate, HRV and skin temperature against your personal baseline and matches the pattern to where you are in your cycle.
One short insight, grounded in research
You get a brief, hedged note with source tags and at most one action, such as a nutrition suggestion for your stage, instead of a dashboard of numbers to interpret. Every claim traces to a PubMed or DOI reference, and a verifier checks each one.
It refuses to send what breaks its own rules
A second model from a different family reviews every draft before it goes out. Unsafe drafts are held, never sent.
Safe by design
No diagnoses, no numeric food targets, no body-composition talk, and escalation triggers that route to real clinical care.
Built so failures improve it
Low-rated insights become new test scenarios. The eval framework is most of the project, with rule checks, single-rule LLM judges and adversarial scenarios.

Fintracker

A finance dashboard and advisor, built in Python with Claude, that runs locally

It connects to your bank through SimpleFIN and your brokerage through SnapTrade, read-only, so every number comes from your real accounts. Net worth, spending and investing live in one place, and the advice follows your own financial goals.

Open the demoRead the code

It is informational only, not financial advice, and it never trades. The demo and the screens below use fake data, and nothing you click leaves the page.

Screens from the demo
Net worth and spending in one view
Balances from every connected account roll up into one net worth, and spending is sorted by category, with unusual charges and recurring subscriptions flagged.
Your data stays with you
There is no account and no cloud. Everything sits in one local file, and the dashboard only runs on your own computer.
The Invest tab gives next steps, not charts
An ordered to-do list of what to buy, trim or hold, how much and by when, shaped by your goals and by economic and political context. Each step shows its reasoning, what would make it wrong, and its sources.
A checker blocks advice that contradicts your data
Selling what you do not own, spending cash you do not have, or selling a gain before it is long-term gets blocked, not listed. Numbers are computed from your accounts, never guessed.
It knows you
One file holds your goals, risk tolerance and things you never want to own, and every answer reads it first.
Ask before you spend
Ask "should I buy a $400 jacket?" and get an answer from your own cash flow, goals and plans.
